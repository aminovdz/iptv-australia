## 4. 5 Essential Fire Stick Optimizations for Australian NBN

Even with the best hardware and streaming service, local Australian network quirks can occasionally cause stutter. Apply these five tweaks inside your Fire Stick settings for maximum performance:

### 1. Increase Player Buffer Size
Inside TiviMate or your player settings:
- Navigate to **Settings** > **Playback** > **Buffer Size**.
- Change the buffer from **None** to **Medium (3-5 Seconds)** or **Large**.
- *Why:* This instructs the Fire Stick to cache several seconds of video in RAM before rendering, completely neutralizing brief local Wi-Fi packet drops.

### 2. Force 5GHz Wi-Fi Frequency
Most standard Australian home routers and Wi-Fi modems combine 2.4GHz and 5GHz into a single network name (Band Steering).
- If possible, separate your Wi-Fi SSIDs in your modem settings and connect your Fire Stick strictly to the **5GHz network**. 5GHz provides significantly higher bandwidth and avoids interference from household appliances.

### 3. Change DNS to Cloudflare (`1.1.1.1`)
Australian ISPs routinely maintain slow recursive DNS servers that delay channel switching.
- In your Fire TV **Network Settings** or your home router settings, set Primary DNS to `1.1.1.1` and Secondary DNS to `1.0.0.1`.
- This ensures channel zapping happens instantaneously in under 500 milliseconds.

### 4. Enable Hardware Video Decoding (HW+)
- In your player's video decoder settings, select **Hardware (HW+)** rather than Software (SW).
- Hardware decoding utilizes the Fire Stick's dedicated graphic processor, reducing device temperature and preventing overheating crashes during extended 4-hour sports broadcasts.

### 5. Periodically Clear Cache Memory
- Go to Fire TV **Settings** > **Applications** > **Manage Installed Applications**.
- Select your IPTV player and click **Clear Cache** (do NOT click *Clear Data*).
- Performing this once a month keeps your Fire Stick running at peak responsiveness.
